RaC: Recovery & Correction for Robot Learning

Robotic Caregiving & Human Interaction Lab, CMU · Research Assistant · Mar – Aug 2025 · Bimanual xArm7 · VR Teleoperation · Imitation Learning

Paper: Zheyuan Hu, Robyn Wu, Naveen Enock, Jasmine Li, Riya Kadakia, Zackory Erickson, Aviral Kumar. RaC: Robot Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks by Scaling Recovery and Correction. Accepted to IEEE Transactions on Robotics (T-RO).
arXiv · PDF · Project page & videos

Overview

Robot policies trained by imitating human demonstrations tend to plateau well short of reliable on long-horizon, contact-rich tasks with deformable objects. Small errors compound, and collecting more “perfect” demonstrations has diminishing returns: even with over 5,000 demonstrations, prior state-of-the-art models hang a shirt successfully only about 75% of the time.

The issue is what's missing from the data. Clean demonstrations rarely show a robot getting out of trouble: in a sample of the large DROID dataset, only 3.68% of episodes contain any recovery behavior. RaC adds a training phase after imitation pre-training that deliberately collects those behaviors.

Method

The policy runs on its own, and when a human operator sees failure coming they take over and do two things:

  • Recover: rewind the robot back to a familiar, in-distribution state.
  • Correct: complete the current sub-task from there.

Two rules shape the data: every intervention pairs a recovery with a correction, and the episode ends right after the intervention so that later sub-tasks aren't recorded under a mix of human and policy behavior. The training objective stays the same as standard imitation learning; the gains come entirely from what the data contains.

System

  • Robot: two 7-DoF xArm7 arms with soft grippers, one overhead camera and two wrist cameras, streaming data at 60 Hz.
  • VR shared autonomy: Oculus Quest controllers with a “clutch” button. Holding it hands control to the operator, releasing it hands control back to the policy. Motions are measured relative to where the controller started, so the operator never has to line their hand up with the gripper.
  • Recovery guidance: SAM2 tracks the grippers in the overhead camera across the original demonstrations to render a heatmap of where the grippers usually are, showing operators where to recover to.
  • Policy: a 300M-parameter multimodal diffusion transformer trained with flow matching, predicting one second of actions at a time and replanning every 0.5 s.

Tasks

  • Shirt hanging (5 steps): lift a hanger off the rack, hand it to the other gripper, insert it into the right collar, then the left collar, and hang the shirt on the rack.
  • Airtight container lid sealing (5 steps): grasp a lid from a dish rack, reorient and place it, snap two opposing tabs, rotate the bowl, snap the last two tabs.
  • Takeout box packing (7 steps): place a clamshell box, pick up a spatula, scoop a burger, put it in the box, nudge it into place, close the lid and tuck in the locking tab.
  • Bimanual assembly (simulation, 3 steps): block into socket, socket onto block, workpiece onto platform.

Results

  • Shirt hanging: 78.3% success with 5 hours of data, compared with 75.0% for ALOHA Unleashed using ~89 hours (5,345 demos) and ~63.6% for Seed GR-3 using ~116 hours.
  • Simulated assembly: 61/100 successes, compared with 22/100 for HG-DAgger and 23/100 for full demonstrations.
  • Up to 10× more data-efficient than prior work, with steeper scaling than collecting full demonstrations or correction-only interventions (HG-DAgger) across all tasks.
  • Test-time scaling: the more recovery maneuvers a policy performs during a rollout, the more likely it is to succeed. Success rises roughly linearly with recoveries (r = 0.71–0.88 across the real-world tasks).
